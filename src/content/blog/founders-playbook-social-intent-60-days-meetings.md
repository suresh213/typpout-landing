## Why Social Intent Beats Traditional Outreach

Most outbound starts with a list. You target companies based on size, industry, or tech stack. Then you blast a cold email or LinkedIn message and hope for a response.

But here’s the flaw: **You’re not timing your outreach to when your prospect is ready.**

Social intent flips this model. Instead of guessing when someone is in-market, you *know*—because you’re monitoring real-time signals across the web. These signals include:

| Signal Type | Example | Relevance Score |
|------------|--------|----------------|
| LinkedIn Engagement | Prospect likes/comments on posts about “scalable CRM” | High |
| Job Postings | “Hiring Sales Ops Manager” with “Salesforce migration” mentioned | Medium-High |
| Forum Activity | Prospect asks in Slack community: “What’s the best ABM tool for 2026?” | Very High |
| Website Activity | Prospect visits pricing page 3 times in one day | High |

Traditional tools give you firmographics. Social intent gives you **behavioral urgency**.

> **Data from Typpout’s GTM engine shows that prospects who exhibit 3+ intent signals convert 3.8x faster** than those with only firmographic matches.

## The 4-Layer Social Intent Framework for Founders

To build a repeatable, scalable motion, use this layered approach:

### 1. **Data Collection: Listen Everywhere, Filter for Action**
You need a real-time data pipeline that captures intent signals across:

- LinkedIn
- Twitter/X
- Reddit (industry subreddits)
- Industry forums (e.g., Indie Hackers, GrowthHackers)
- Website behavior (via tools like Typpout’s AI waterfall)

**Action Step:**
✅ Use a tool like Typpout to unify intent data into a single dashboard.
✅ Filter for **high-intent signals**:
  - Multiple visits to pricing pages
  - Engagement with competitor content
  - Job postings for roles you solve
  - Social shares of “future of [your category]”

> *Pro Tip:* Founders should prioritize signals from **decision-makers** (e.g., CROs posting about “scaling sales teams”). These have the highest ROI.

### 2. **Scoring: Assign Weight to Signals**
Not all intent is equal. Create a simple scoring model:

| Signal | Weight |
|-------|--------|
| Prospect visits pricing page 3x+ in 48h | 10 |
| Prospect engages with your LinkedIn post | 8 |
| Prospect posts job ad for role you solve | 9 |
| Prospect visits competitor’s pricing page | 7 |

**Action Step:**
✅ Set up automated scoring in your GTM stack.
✅ Trigger alerts when a prospect hits a threshold (e.g., 20+ points).
✅ Prioritize top 10% of intent-rich leads weekly.

### 3. **Personalization: Stop “Hi [First Name]”—Start “I saw you just asked about [Pain Point]”**
The most powerful outreach leverages **real-time context**.

Example:
> “Hi Alex,
> I noticed you just posted on LinkedIn about struggling to align sales and marketing during expansion. We helped 50+ B2B teams do exactly that—without adding headcount. Can I share a 2-minute case study?”

That message gets **7x higher reply rates** than generic cold outreach.

**Action Step:**
✅ Use intent data to craft **hyper-personalized hooks**.
✅ Reference specific signals: “I saw you downloaded the ‘2026 Sales Tech Stack Guide’—I wrote a follow-up on how top teams optimize spend.”
✅ Keep messages under 50 words. Founders don’t have time for long emails.

### 4. **Automation: Reply, Engage, Book—Without the Busywork**
The best founders don’t spend hours typing messages. They use AI to:
- Detect intent signals in real time
- Draft personalized replies
- Handle initial conversations
- Book meetings automatically

**Typpout’s GTM engine**, for example, uses AI to:

- Book demo calls directly from Slack or inbox

### Week 1–2: Setup & Baseline
- [ ] Integrate LinkedIn, Twitter, and website tracking
- [ ] Define high-intent keywords (e.g., “best CRM for mid-market,” “how to scale sales team”)
- [ ] Set up scoring model and alert thresholds
- [ ] Train AI reply templates based on your voice

### Week 3–4: Launch & Learn
- [ ] Run a 14-day pilot: target 50 high-intent prospects
- [ ] A/B test messaging variants (e.g., “I noticed…” vs. “We help…”)
- [ ] Measure reply rates, meeting bookings, and time-to-first-reply
