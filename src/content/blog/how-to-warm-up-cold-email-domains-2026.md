Domain warmup is a critical first step for any cold email campaign. If you send emails from a new domain without warming it up first, email providers will flag your account as spam, destroying your deliverability.

## 1. Set Up DNS Records

Before sending any emails, you must configure your DNS settings:
* **SPF (Sender Policy Framework):** Specifies which mail servers are authorized to send email on behalf of your domain.
* **DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail):** Adds a digital signature to your emails, verifying that they haven't been altered.
* **DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication):** Instructs receiving mail servers how to handle emails that fail SPF or DKIM checks.

## 2. Gradual Volume Ramp

Start by sending a few emails per day and slowly increase the volume over 4-6 weeks. Use automated warmup tools to simulate realistic email conversations and build a positive sending history.
